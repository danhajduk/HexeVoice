Voice identity and audio-quality architecture

Each voice endpoint captures an utterance and sends the same audio to the node. On the node, three analysis paths run in parallel:

STT — converts speech to text.
Speaker ID — identifies the likely person speaking and returns a confidence score.
Audio quality / environment analysis — measures background noise, SNR, clipping, speech duration, overlap, and optionally classifies things like TV, music, appliances, or other speech.

The results are then combined before intent execution.

Speaker identification behavior

Speaker identity is only required for intents that need a person context, such as calendars, email, personal data, music profiles, or other user-specific services.

Generic intents do not need speaker identification and can execute normally without storing or caring who spoke.

Speaker-confidence handling should be tiered:

High confidence: trusted enough to identify the user and potentially use that interaction to improve the stored voice profile.
Medium confidence: okay for low-risk personalization, but do not learn from it.
Low confidence / unknown: do not guess. If identity is required, Hexe asks who is speaking or requests confirmation.

Exact thresholds should be configurable and calibrated from real household data rather than hard-coded permanently.

Profile learning should also require a clear margin between the best and second-best speaker matches to prevent profile drift.

Voice enrollment

Initial speaker enrollment should use roughly 8–12 natural phrases, around 30–60 seconds total speech per person.

The phrases should vary in:

length
phonetics
question vs command structure
normal speaking rhythm

Enrollment should ideally happen across multiple short sessions or slightly different positions so the system learns the person rather than one specific microphone condition.

Store multiple representative speaker embeddings, not just one single sample.

Audio quality and background-noise analysis

The speaker model itself generally will not explain why confidence is low, so audio-quality analysis should be a separate parallel component.

Useful signals include:

signal-to-noise ratio
RMS / speech level
clipping
speech duration
overlapping speakers
background speech
music or TV-like audio
general environmental sound classification

Possible local tools/models discussed were things like:

WebRTC audio processing
RNNoise
Silero VAD
YAMNet or similar AudioSet-based classifiers
pyannote for overlap / diarization if needed later

The combined result could explain things like:

speaker unknown
low SNR
speech too short
multiple speakers detected
TV/background speech likely present
Pre-roll and ambient noise

When the wake word triggers, the endpoint should preserve a short pre-roll buffer before the actual utterance.

That pre-speech segment can be used as the ambient-noise reference for that interaction.

Then the node compares ambient level against speech level to estimate SNR without needing a continuous audio stream.

Multiple endpoints hearing the same wake word

If two nearby endpoints detect the same wake word, they should not both automatically stream the full utterance.

Instead, use a short election window where endpoints report metrics such as:

wake-word confidence
speech level
SNR
possibly microphone quality

The node selects the best endpoint and tells the others to stand down.

If two endpoints are extremely close in quality, both could briefly stream the first part of the utterance and one could then be dropped, but that should be the exception.

Placement calibration mode

A separate calibration mode can help determine the best physical placement of a voice endpoint.

During installation or testing, the endpoint can periodically sample ambient audio, for example every 10 minutes over 24–48 hours.

For those periodic background samples, run:

ambient noise level
SNR-related metrics
event/environment classification
speech-like activity detection

Do not normally run full STT or speaker identification on unattended periodic samples.

That avoids unnecessary transcription, privacy concerns, and accidental speaker-profile learning from TV or background conversations.

The raw audio can be discarded after extracting metrics.

Active placement testing

For a proper voice-performance test, someone can intentionally trigger a placement test and speak known phrases from normal locations in the room.

Those samples should run through the full pipeline:

STT
speaker ID
audio-quality analysis

Because the expected phrase and speaker are known, Hexe can measure:

STT accuracy
speaker-ID reliability
SNR
environmental contamination
response consistency from different positions
Placement report

The final endpoint placement report could combine:

average ambient noise by time of day
peak noise periods
frequency of TV/music/background speech
SNR distribution
STT success rate during active tests
speaker-ID confidence/reliability
overlapping-speech frequency
overall placement score

That gives a much more useful answer than just “this spot is loud.” It tells you whether the device actually performs well for voice interaction in that location.

Future, not active scope

Continuous audio monitoring could later support things like:

smoke/fire alarm detection
glass breaking
baby crying
unusual sound events
